# VetCrew — Concept Presentation Blueprint (self-contained, for the clinical partner)

**Purpose:** the blueprint for the presentation Dan will build in Claude Design to bring VetCrew, as a concept, to the hospital's one clinically-credentialed technician — the person whose sign-off the product depends on. This document is the *brief* (structure + message + screen manifest); Claude Design turns it into the deck.

**Hard constraints (from Dan):**
- **Self-contained. There is NO live pitch.** Every slide must carry its own meaning with zero narration — she may go through it alone, or it may circulate. No "Dan explains slide 4." If a slide needs a talk-track, it's not done.
- **Language: Hebrew.** She and the crew work in Hebrew; the product is Hebrew-first. UI screens stay in their real Hebrew/RTL + Latin clinical abbreviations (HR / SpO₂ / MAP). (She's English-fluent, so this is a *fit* choice, not a hard limit.)
- **The one thing she must believe by the end:** *her clinical expertise is the product — she is the irreplaceable authority it can't exist without — and she wants in.* Every slide serves that arc.
- **Hero screens: ~4–6, and the narrative below names exactly which.** No screen appears that the story doesn't need.

**Tone:** calm, clinical, credible, respectful of her expertise. Not salesy, not hypey — a skeptical, clinically-literate reader must find nothing to dismiss. It should read the way the product looks: instrument-grade, serious, quiet confidence.

---

## The narrative arc (why this order)

Problem she *feels* → the gap nobody fills → the concept (built on what she already does) → **see it** → the decision layer (where a clinician leans in) → the evidence it produces → why it's defensible → **her role is the keystone** → the ask. The emotional turn is late and deliberate: she should be nodding at the problem and intrigued by the solution *before* the deck reveals that she is the missing piece.

---

## Slide-by-slide

Each slide: **purpose · Hebrew headline (draft) · the self-contained message · visual/screen · design note.** Headlines are drafts to finalize with the `hebrew-content-writer` skill; the *message* is the working spec.

### 1 — Title / hook
- **Purpose:** name it and set the register in one breath.
- **Headline (he) — Dan's copy (authoritative):** «VetCrew — הכשרת צוות למחלקות חירום וטרינריות» · sub: «איך נדע שטכנאי חדש מוכן להתחיל לעבוד?»
  *(VetCrew — crew training for veterinary ER departments. "How will we know a new technician is ready to start working?")*
- **Message:** one line stating what it is (a simulator + assessment for ER/ICU technicians) and the question it answers. The sub-question is the hook — it's the question the hospital can't currently answer.
- **Visual:** the VetCrew wordmark on the dark-instrument background; a hint of the monitor behind it. Quiet, premium.
- **Design note:** no bullet points ever on the title. One line, one question.

### 2 — The problem, in her world
- **Purpose:** make her feel the pain before any solution — honestly, in her own reality.
- **Copy (he) — Dan's, authoritative:**
  «מה קורה היום? איך יודעים שטכנאי חדש יכול להתחיל לעבוד? התשובה — בעיקר תחושת בטן מצד הטכנאי החופף והמבחן שלך. וגם, הכי חשוב, לחץ מצד ההנהלה להעסיק טכנאים חדשים בגלל מחסור.»
  *(What happens today? How do we know a new technician can start working? The answer — mostly gut feeling from the handover technician, and your exam. And, most importantly, pressure from management to hire new technicians because of the shortage.)*
- **Why this copy is strong (do not dilute it):** it is honest and it is *hers*. Three true things stacked: (1) assessment today = the handover tech's **gut feeling**, (2) **"your exam"** — she is already the assessor; the current written test is hers, (3) **management pressure to hire anyway** because of the shortage. That last one is the trap: weak assessment *and* forced hiring = risk carried onto the floor. Nobody's fault — the *tools* are inadequate.
- **STRATEGIC NOTE — this reframes the whole deck for the better.** "המבחן שלך" means **she is already the clinical assessor** at this hospital, holding the line with a written exam and gut feel. So VetCrew is **not replacing her judgment — it is the instrument she's never had for the job she already does.** This makes slide 10's keystone land far harder: she isn't being recruited into a new role, she's being handed a better tool for the one she already owns. Carry this thread from here to slide 10.
- **Visual:** understated. The three realities (gut feel · the exam · hiring pressure) as three quiet lines, not a dramatic graphic. No screens yet.
- **Design note:** tone is honest, never blaming. The exam is hers and it's inadequate *because a written test cannot capture the floor* — that's a limit of the method, not of her. She should read this and feel *understood*, not judged.

### 3 — Why it matters (the cost)
- **Purpose:** the problem isn't just annoying; it's expensive and systemic.
- **Headline (he):** «התחלופה במחלקות חירום מגיעה ל-79%. אין אונבורדינג סטנדרטי.»
- **Message:** ER/specialty technician turnover runs ~79% (Instinct Science, 2024); much of the field is uncredentialed; there is no standardised onboarding. Every unassessed hire is a risk carried onto the floor. *(Cite sources on-slide — credibility.)*
- **Visual:** two or three clean stat tiles (dark-instrument style, per the dataviz discipline — restrained).
- **Design note:** numbers are tabular/mono, quiet, sourced. This is the "this is real" evidence slide — no adjectives, just figures with citations.

### 4 — The gap nobody fills
- **Purpose:** position — why this doesn't already exist.
- **Headline (he):** «יש מי שמסמן וי על מטלות. אין מי שמודד החלטות.»
  *(Some tools tick off tasks. None measure decisions.)*
- **Message:** existing tools either score a **technical checklist** (commoditised — anyone does it) or are built for **human medicine / CPR only**. **Nobody scores the everyday job *and* the decisions inside it** — which route, which tube, when to escalate — and nobody produces a defensible record of it. That whitespace is the product.
- **Visual:** a simple 2×2 or a "what exists vs. what's missing" contrast. Minimal.
- **Design note:** this is where the clinically-literate reader starts to lean in — "decisions, not just tasks" is the hook for *her* specifically.

### 5 — The concept: a competency mountain, and we start at the base
- **Purpose:** the solution, framed as something she already understands.
- **Headline (he) — Dan's copy:** «הר הכשירות — מהמטלה היומיומית ועד החייאה מרובת-מטופלים.»
  *(The competency mountain — from the everyday task to multi-patient CPR.)*
- **Message:** VetCrew is a progression — a mountain. The **base** is everyday floor competence (one technician, real tasks); the **summit** is a full crew running multi-patient CPR. We ship **one base rung first**. And it's built on the mental model the crew already uses every shift — the **colour-coded task board** (do / report / timed / needs-approval) — so it asks almost no new behaviour. *(This is the near-zero-friction point — critical.)*
- **Visual:** a simple **mountain / progression diagram** (base rung → … → summit), with the base rung highlighted as "what we build first." *(This is the one net-new visual — see Screen Manifest; it can be a clean diagram, not a UI screen.)*
- **Design note:** the mountain makes the vision legible in one image while making clear you're not boiling the ocean — you ship the base.

### 6 — See it: the technician's station
- **Purpose:** the "physically see what it looks like" payoff. This is the slide that makes it real.
- **Headline (he):** «ככה זה נראה — מוניטור אמיתי, ומשימות אמיתיות לצידו.»
- **Message:** this is what the technician sees: a **faithful patient monitor** (the real Mindray channel colours and waveforms) beside the **task panel** for a hospitalised patient. They read the monitor, perform the tasks, enter values — exactly the shift, simulated.
- **Visual:** **HERO — the base-rung split-screen** (task panel + patient monitor). The centrepiece of the whole deck.
- **Design note:** let the screen breathe — large, minimal chrome around it. This slide sells itself if the screen is good.

### 7 — The decision layer (where a clinician leans in)
- **Purpose:** the intellectual core — this tests judgment, not memorisation. Aimed squarely at *her*.
- **Headline (he):** «לא רק "עשית" — אלא "עשית נכון": מנה, נתיב, והחלטה מתי לעצור.»
  *(Not just "did you" — "did you do it right": dose, route, and the decision when to stop.)*
- **Message:** a medication task doesn't just ask the volume — it scores **dose *and* route separately**. Giving the right millilitres by the wrong route (an SC-only drug pushed IV) is a **fatal error, and it's scored as a fail, not partial credit.** The system never computes the answer and never blocks the mistake — because the mistake is the measurement. And it tests the hardest thing: *did you notice the abnormality and escalate, instead of pushing on?*
- **Visual:** **HERO — the medication task with the route selector**, showing the wrong-route-logged state (the diphenhydramine-IV example). *(Already built and verified.)*
- **Design note:** this is the slide that earns a skeptical clinician's respect. The example must be clinically real (it is). This is where "someone finally gets it" lands.

### 8 — The result: a real test, safely, that can actually be reviewed
- **Purpose:** what the assessment gives you — and why it's trustworthy. *(Dan's copy shifted this slide's emphasis from "a record" to the assessment itself: realistic, safe, reviewable. Honoured below.)*
- **Copy (he) — Dan's, authoritative:**
  «התוצאה: הטכנאי נבחן בתנאי לחץ שמדמים מציאות אמיתית, שאפשר להעביר עליה ביקורת אמיתית — וללא סכנה על בעלי חיים אמיתיים.»
  *(The result: the technician is tested under pressure conditions that simulate real reality, that can be genuinely reviewed — and with no risk to real animals.)*
- **Three value pillars in this line (all real):** (1) **realistic pressure** — not a written test, the actual shift simulated; (2) **reviewable** — and it's reviewable *because* every action is recorded and every score traces back to the exact moment that produced it (the mechanism behind "ביקורת אמיתית"); (3) **no risk to real animals** — today a new tech's mistakes happen *on live patients*; here they happen in simulation, where failing is safe. That safety argument lands hard with a clinician. **Elevate pillar 3 — it wasn't foregrounded before and it's one of the strongest.**
- **Visual:** **HERO — the AAR / scored record screen** (timeline + expected-vs-actual + a score visibly linked to its event). The screen is what makes "reviewable" concrete.
- **Design note:** show the traceability visibly — a score connected to its source moment. That connection *is* the "ביקורת אמיתית." *(Screen needs finishing — see Manifest.)*

### 9 — Why it's defensible (clinical accuracy is a hard gate)
- **Purpose:** pre-empt the skeptic's biggest objection — "is the clinical content right?"
- **Headline (he):** «שום דבר לא מנקד אף אחד לפני אישור קליני מול תקן.»
  *(Nothing scores anyone before clinical sign-off against a standard.)*
- **Message:** clinical accuracy isn't polish — it's a hard gate. No scenario scores a real person until every clinical claim in it is **checked against a published standard** (RECOVER, the CVTEA skills list, the drug's own label). It's built into the system so it's *structurally impossible* to score on unreviewed content.
- **Visual:** a simple "sign-off gate" motif — claim → checked against [standard] → stamped. Restrained.
- **Design note:** this slide is the bridge to slide 10. It establishes that clinical authority is central and non-negotiable — which sets up *who provides it.*

### 10 — The keystone: her expertise is the product *(the emotional turn)*
- **Purpose:** the reveal. Everything has built to this.
- **Headline (he):** «הידע הקליני הוא המוצר. בלעדיו — אין כלום.»
  *(The clinical knowledge is the product. Without it — there's nothing.)*
- **Message:** the thing that makes VetCrew defensible and real is not the software — it's the **clinical judgment encoded in it.** The scored decisions come from **real floor failures**: the SC-only drug given IV; reaching for IV access before airway and pulses. Those are not content you can download — they are **IP, drawn from clinical expertise.** The whole system is only as good as the clinician who validates it — and it *cannot exist* without one. That person, here, is **you.** Not a rubber stamp — the authority the product is built around.
- **Visual:** quiet and human. Perhaps the two observed-failure examples rendered simply, credited as "the kind of judgment that becomes the product." Minimal, weighty.
- **Design note:** this is the slide that must land the "I want in." No hype — respect. She should feel *seen* as the expert, and see that her knowledge has a place to live.

### 11 — What being the clinical authority means (the ask, made concrete and bounded)
- **Purpose:** turn the feeling into a defined, non-scary role.
- **Headline (he):** «מה זה אומר בפועל — תפקיד מוגדר, מגובה בתקנים, מתועד בשמך.»
- **Message:** the role is bounded and defensible: **confirm each clinical claim against a named standard** (not "in your opinion" — *against RECOVER / CVTEA / the label*), and it's recorded in your name and the scenario version. You're not inventing content or carrying open-ended liability — you're the citable authority who verifies it. It starts small: one base-rung scenario.
- **Visual:** a clean "your workflow" strip — claim → you confirm against [standard] → stamped & credited. 
- **Design note:** this de-risks the ask. A clinician's fear is unbounded liability; this slide answers it before she asks.

### 12 — Close: the mountain, and her at the centre of it
- **Purpose:** end on the vision, with her in it.
- **Headline (he):** «מתחילים ברגל אחת של ההר. יחד.»
  *(We start with one rung of the mountain. Together.)*
- **Message:** restate the vision — the mountain — and that it starts now, with one base rung, built on her clinical authority. One clear next step (a conversation).
- **Visual:** the mountain diagram from slide 5, now with "you are here / we start here" and a horizon.
- **Design note:** quiet, forward-looking. Not a hard CTA — an invitation.
